Four competencies, one loop

AI Fluency means working with AI effectively, efficiently, ethically and safely. Developed by Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller with Anthropic, the framework rests on four core competencies.

Delegation

What the AI handles, and what stays human

Plan the work, then divide it.

Plan first whenever a task has 3+ steps, or if redoing it carries high cost.

Description

Stating clearly what is needed

Define the output, the method and the behaviour.

Discernment

Is the generated output any good?

Check the result, the reasoning and the exchange.

Describe → Discern → Refine → Decide

Description and Discernment iterate until the output is right.

Diligence

Is it being used responsibly?

Protect data, disclose the AI's role, own the result.

The same request, three depths

The fluency-levels skill clarifies more upfront when the stakes are higher. Toggle the depth to compare.

1 round · 3 questions

The AI makes most decisions.

It asks, for example

  1. What is this page for, and what does success look like?
  2. Format, length, and who will read it?
  3. Anything it must never do, or must include?

Concludes with two or three "Verify" flags on points most prone to error.

Build an AI Fluency page.

2 rounds · 7 questions

Decisions are shared, requiring an initial outline to be approved.

It asks, for example

  1. Which parts stay with you, and which go to the AI?
  2. What should be attached or known before starting?
  3. Narrow or explore? Challenge or follow? Detailed or concise?

Closes with a red-flag check on names, numbers, dates and absolute claims.

3 rounds · 13 questions

The human makes the key decisions, while the AI challenges the plan.

It asks, for example

  1. Which parts need human judgment and must stay with you?
  2. Is this a familiar field? If not, a short coaching round comes first.
  3. Who will see the result, and do they need to know AI helped?

Ends with a critical challenge, a staged plan with checkpoints, and a 4D self-grade.

How to install

Claude apps

Turn on code execution in Settings > Capabilities. Then open Customize > Skills, choose + and Create skill, upload the .zip and switch it on.

Claude Code

Unzip into ~/.claude/skills/ for every project, or into .claude/skills/ for one. Then type /prompt-4d-review or /fluency-levels.

Antigravity

Unzip into .agents/skills/ in the workspace, or into ~/.gemini/config/skills/ for every project. Then type /fluency-levels, or simply ask.

How it was made

Two AI tools and one human directing decisions. Each step below shows who did the work.

  1. Human

    Working through the course lesson by lesson, noting key insights along the way.

  2. Together

    Claude parsed each lesson into structured notes, while choosing what mattered remained human.

  3. Together

    Claude drafted the content and layouts. Final design rules and the sign-off on each sheet remained human.

  4. AI

    Gemini rewrote the copy, while Claude verified every line against the course material for meaning.

  5. Human

    Copy and formatting adjusted by hand until every sheet read cleanly on the wall.

Where it lives

Printed sheets pinned beside the desk, ready for a quick glance while working with AI.

fluency-levels is built for important tasks: Quick handles smaller jobs, while Deep serves work that is costly to redo.

prompt-4d-review is built for a single moment: verifying a prompt before hitting send.

How AI was used

I made these cheat sheets and skills while taking Anthropic Academy's AI Fluency: Framework and foundations course, working with two AI tools:

  • Claude (Anthropic): read the course pages and transcripts, kept structured notes, proposed the content, and built the layouts and the two skills.
  • Gemini (Google, in Antigravity): paraphrased the sheet text so it reads naturally.

Icons come from open-source sets (Phosphor, Lucide, Tabler).

I chose what goes on each sheet, set the design rules, and wrote or rewrote key phrases. I reviewed every sheet and every line by hand. Claude checked each paraphrased line against the course material for meaning. The final result reflects my understanding of the framework and my own choices, and I take full responsibility for its content, accuracy and presentation.

This page was designed and created with Claude using this exact process; I made the decisions and reviewed the result.

I'm sharing this disclosure because transparency about AI's role is one of the principles the course teaches.
